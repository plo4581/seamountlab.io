Field challenges — West Europe

Each challenge is tied to a location. Go there. The goal is observation — of the environment, the organisms, the conditions. Document what you find and share it with us. Points are logged. We'll figure out what they mean together.

C-001
The fog line
Find the altitude where fog forms in your nearest mountain range. Document the transition — what grows on one side of the line that doesn't grow on the other. Photograph the surface of anything that collects water without soil.

C-002
Below the canopy
Run or walk from open ground into dense forest cover. Time how long your body takes to thermoregulate after the transition. Document the temperature difference if you can. Note what you notice about the organisms living at the boundary.

C-003
The intertidal zone
Find a rocky coastline at low tide. Spend at least 30 minutes looking at organisms that live in the intertidal zone — the strip that's underwater half the time and exposed the other half. How do they attach? What happens to them when the water recedes?
60
points
Atlantic / North Sea coast

C-004
Above the treeline
Get to an altitude above where trees grow. Document what does grow there and how. Look at how plants deal with exposure — wind, UV, temperature swings. Photograph any organism that looks like it shouldn't be able to survive where it is.

C-005
The night run
Run somewhere familiar at night. Document what you notice that you don't notice in daylight — sounds, temperature, your own body's response to low light. Optional: photograph any bioluminescence you find (fungi, sea life, insects).

C-006
Water in motion
Find a river, stream or waterfall. Look at what lives in or around fast-moving water. How do organisms anchor themselves against the current? What shapes recur? Spend time looking at the surface tension, not just the organisms.

C-007
Extreme dry
Find the driest environment within reach — a south-facing limestone escarpment, a rain shadow valley, a coastal dune system. Document what survives there and how it manages water. Look for structures on leaf or stem surfaces.

The long exposure
Spend at least three hours in one place outside without moving significantly. Document what changes around you over time — light, temperature, animal behaviour, your own body's response to sustained stillness in an outdoor environment.
